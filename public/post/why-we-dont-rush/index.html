Every week, someone asks me why Mare e Terra doesn’t publish more often. Why no daily newsletter. Why no Instagram Stories at 7 PM sharp. Why our issues arrive when they arrive — and not a moment sooner.

The short answer is this: the Mediterranean taught me to wait.

I grew up watching my grandmother move through a kitchen that had no timer but the sun. She didn't check a clock to know when the bread was done — she listened to it. The crust told her. The smell told her. The way the light fell across the counter told her.

This is not nostalgia. This is a methodology.

The world of food media — and media in general — has become a machine that must be fed. Content every day, every hour, every platform, every trend. The pressure to produce is constant, and the result is that most of what gets published is noise. It is written because a calendar said it must be written, not because there was something worth saying.

We do not want to be noise.

The Economy of Attention

There's a well-known fallacy in publishing that says: more content equals more growth. Feed the algorithm, and the algorithm will feed you. It works, for a while. But it creates a strange paradox — the more you publish, the less each piece matters. Readers stop reading. They scroll. They skim.

We are choosing a different bet. We believe that when you publish something worth reading — truly worth reading — people will make time for it. Not because we've tricked them into clicking, but because the writing itself rewards their attention.

This means we say no to a lot. No to the SEO-optimized listicle about "10 Mediterranean Superfoods You Need Now." No to the rushed recipe because it's Tuesday and we "have to post something." No to the hot take on a food trend that will be forgotten by next season.

Instead, we wait until we have something real. A conversation with a producer in the Zadar hinterland. A tasting note that took three bottles to get right. A season — like this one, wild asparagus and early oil — that deserves more than a caption.

Slow Publishing as Resistance

I think of what we're doing as a small act of resistance against the culture of speed. The same way the slow food movement pushed back against fast food, we are pushing back against fast publishing. It is not anti-technology — we love that you can read this on a phone or a laptop or folded awkwardly into a coat pocket at the market. It is anti-chaos.

A friend in publishing once told me: "The internet is a library where someone is constantly screaming." I want Mare e Terra to be the quiet corner of that library. The one with the good light. The one where you sit down and forget the time.

This is not a moral stance. It is a practical one. We are a small team. We do not have a content factory. What we have is time — time to research, to taste, to write, to edit. And if we use that time well, the result will be worth more than a hundred hurried posts.

What You Can Expect

So here is the honest truth: we will not publish on a fixed schedule. We will publish when we have something that meets our standard. Sometimes that will be weekly. Sometimes it will be every ten days. Occasionally — if the season is slow, or the writing needs more time — you might wait two weeks.

And that's okay. Because when the next issue arrives, it will be worth the wait.

The olive tree doesn't fruit every week. The sea doesn't give up its best fish on demand. And Mare e Terra will keep following the same rhythm — the one the Mediterranean has always known.

Slowly. Deliberately. When the time is right.

— Elena
